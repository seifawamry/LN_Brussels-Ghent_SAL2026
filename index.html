Tax Refund (VAT) Process

Comprehensive guide for Egyptian physicians claiming VAT refunds at Brussels Airport (BRU)

Save up to 21% VAT

Belgium Tax-Free Shopping Guidelines

As non-EU residents traveling from Egypt, delegates are legally entitled to claim back the Value Added Tax (VAT / BTW / TVA) paid on personal retail purchases made in Belgium when exporting those goods within 3 months.

Min Spend: €50.01 per store / day
Standard Belgian VAT: 21%
Mandatory Customs Stamp at BRU
Net Refund: Approx. 12% – 16%

4 Essential Steps to Claim Your Refund

1
In Stores

Request Tax Free Form

At checkout in Brussels / Ghent boutiques
  • Tell the cashier: "Tax Free form, please" before paying.
  • Present your Egyptian passport (or clear phone photo).
  • Ensure the official till receipt is firmly stapled to the form.
  • Check your full name and Egyptian address are accurately listed.
Supported at City2, Rue Neuve, and Veldstraat boutiques.
2
Packing

Organize Goods & Forms

Saturday night / Sunday morning
  • Keep goods unused in their original store packaging with labels.
  • Decide whether goods will be in Hold Luggage or Cabin Baggage.
  • Hold luggage items must be stamped before check-in bag drop.
  • Keep all receipt envelopes together in your travel wallet.
Put liquids (perfumes, cosmetics) in hold suitcases.
3
BRU Airport

Belgian Customs Validation

Level 3 Departures Hall
  • Check in with EgyptAir MS 726 first; retain tagged bags.
  • Walk with tagged suitcase to Customs Desk (Douane) on Level 3.
  • Present: Passport, Boarding Pass, Tax Free Forms, and Suitcase.
  • Customs officer scans or stamps forms, then takes your suitcase.
No Customs Stamp = Zero Refund!
4
Cash / Card

Collect Your Payout

Refund desk or postbox
  • Immediate Cash: Travelex / Global Blue counter (EUR payout).
  • Credit Card: Write credit card number, seal in operator envelope, drop in airport mailbox.
  • Card refunds arrive within 7 to 14 business days.
  • Snap a smartphone photo of each stamped form before posting!
Card refunds avoid the local cash payout fee.

Belgian Culinary Specialties Guide

Belgian Waffles (Gaufres)

Two styles: Liège Waffle (dense, chewy, caramelized pearl sugar) vs Brussels Waffle (light, rectangular, crispy). Both naturally vegetarian/halal.

Belgian Chocolates & Pralines

World-renowned master chocolatiers (Neuhaus, Mary, Pierre Marcolini). Check ingredient labels to avoid liqueur fillings ("sans alcool").

Gentse Neuzekes (Cuberdons)

Traditional cone-shaped purple raspberry candies unique to Ghent, with a soft liquid syrup core. Available from street carts at Groentenmarkt.

Brussels Architectural Highlights

UNESCO World Heritage • Day 1

Grand-Place (Grote Markt)

Regarded as one of the most beautiful town squares in the world. Surrounded by 17th-century baroque guildhalls with gilded ornamentation, the 15th-century Gothic Town Hall, and King's House.

Open in Maps
1958 World's Fair • Day 2

Atomium

Built for Expo 58, this 102-meter futuristic monument depicts an iron crystal magnified 165 billion times. Features 9 interconnected stainless steel spheres offering sweeping vistas of Brussels.

Open in Maps
Monarchy Heritage • Day 2

Royal Palace of Brussels

The official palace of the King and Queen of the Belgians, situated opposite Brussels Park. Admire the neoclassical facade and the historic Place des Palais administrative district.

Open in Maps
Folkloric Bronze • Day 2

Manneken Pis

Brussels' quirky, world-famous bronze fountain mascot. Stroll through the surrounding historic cobblestone alleys filled with artisan chocolate boutiques and warm waffle parlors.

Ghent Medieval Masterpieces • Day 3 Excursion

12th Century Fortress

Gravensteen (Castle of the Counts)

Dramatic moated castle built by Count Philip of Alsace in 1180. Features massive ramparts, gatehouse, keep, and panoramic views overlooking Ghent's medieval rooftops.

Open in Maps
Van Eyck Masterpiece

St. Bavo's Cathedral (Sint-Baafs)

Imposing Gothic cathedral housing the renowned 1432 Ghent Altarpiece (*The Adoration of the Mystic Lamb*) by Hubert & Jan van Eyck, celebrated as the dawn of the Northern Renaissance.

Open in Maps
Historic Harbor Quays

Graslei & Korenlei

Picturesque medieval quays lined with guild houses dating from the 12th to 17th centuries. One of Europe's most photographed historical riverfront panoramas.
