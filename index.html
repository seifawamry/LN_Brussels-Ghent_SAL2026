Tax Refund (VAT) Process

Comprehensive guide for Egyptian physicians claiming VAT refunds at Brussels Airport (BRU)

Save up to 21% VAT

Belgium Tax-Free Shopping Guidelines

As non-EU residents traveling from Egypt, delegates are legally entitled to claim back the Value Added Tax (VAT / BTW / TVA) paid on personal retail purchases made in Belgium when exporting those goods within 3 months.

Min Spend: €50.01 per store / day
Standard Belgian VAT: 21%
Mandatory Customs Stamp at BRU
Net Refund: Approx. 12% – 16%

4 Essential Steps to Claim Your Refund

1
In Stores

Request Tax Free Form

At checkout in Brussels / Ghent boutiques
  • Tell the cashier: "Tax Free form, please" before paying.
  • Present your Egyptian passport (or clear phone photo).
  • Ensure the official till receipt is firmly stapled to the form.
  • Check your full name and Egyptian address are accurately listed.
Supported at City2, Rue Neuve, and Veldstraat boutiques.
2
Packing

Organize Goods & Forms

Saturday night / Sunday morning
  • Keep goods unused in their original store packaging with labels.
  • Decide whether goods will be in Hold Luggage or Cabin Baggage.
  • Hold luggage items must be stamped before check-in bag drop.
  • Keep all receipt envelopes together in your travel wallet.
Put liquids (perfumes, cosmetics) in hold suitcases.
3
BRU Airport

Belgian Customs Validation

Level 3 Departures Hall
  • Check in with EgyptAir MS 726 first; retain tagged bags.
  • Walk with tagged suitcase to Customs Desk (Douane) on Level 3.
  • Present: Passport, Boarding Pass, Tax Free Forms, and Suitcase.
  • Customs officer scans or stamps forms, then takes your suitcase.
No Customs Stamp = Zero Refund!
4
Cash / Card

Collect Your Payout

Refund desk or postbox
  • Immediate Cash: Travelex / Global Blue counter (EUR payout).
  • Credit Card: Write credit card number, seal in operator envelope, drop in airport mailbox.
  • Card refunds arrive within 7 to 14 business days.
  • Snap a smartphone photo of each stamped form before posting!
Card refunds avoid the local cash payout fee.

Belgian Culinary Specialties Guide

Belgian Waffles (Gaufres)

Two styles: Liège Waffle (dense, chewy, caramelized pearl sugar) vs Brussels Waffle (light, rectangular, crispy). Both naturally vegetarian/halal.

Belgian Chocolates & Pralines

World-renowned master chocolatiers (Neuhaus, Mary, Pierre Marcolini). Check ingredient labels to avoid liqueur fillings ("sans alcool").

Traditional cone-shaped purple raspberry candies unique to Ghent, with a soft liquid syrup core. Available from street carts at Groentenmarkt.

Electric Appliances & Power Compatibility (Belgium vs. Egypt)

100% Voltage Safe
Egypt Power Standard 220 Volts • 50 Hz Sockets: Type C & Type F
Fully Compatible Voltage No Transformer Required
Belgium Power Standard 230 Volts • 50 Hz Sockets: Type E (French standard)

Voltage & Frequency Verdict for Egyptian Doctors:

The voltage difference between Egypt (220V) and Belgium (230V) is within the standard European ±10% grid tolerance. All Egyptian consumer electronics and appliances operate safely and efficiently without any step-down/step-up converter. This includes smartphone chargers (Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi), laptops (Dell, HP, Lenovo, MacBook), tablets, medical devices, camera battery chargers, electric shavers, and curling irons.

Type C (Europlug) Direct Fit • No Adapter

Smartphone & Tablet Chargers

Standard 2-pin flat or round plugs used on Egyptian phone and tablet chargers.

Plugs directly into Renaissance Brussels wall sockets without any adapter!
Type E/F Hybrid (CEE 7/7) Direct Fit • Grounded

Laptop & Heavy Appliance Plugs

Round heavy-duty 2-pin plugs with an earth hole at the top (used on almost all modern laptops in Egypt).

Plugs directly into Belgian sockets — the Belgian wall earth pin inserts smoothly into the plug's top hole!
Type G (UK / Gulf 3-Pin) Adapter Required

UK / Gulf Rectangular 3-Pin Plugs

Plugs with 3 rectangular pins (appliances bought in the UK, Saudi Arabia, or UAE).

Requires a UK-to-EU adapter. Standard Belgian sockets do not accept rectangular pins.

Cards Accepted Universally

Visa and Mastercard (credit, debit, and prepaid cards) are accepted virtually everywhere in Belgium, including convenience stores, taxis, and pharmacies. Contactless payment (tap-to-pay via card or Apple Pay / Google Pay) is the nationwide standard.

Inform Your Egyptian Bank

Prior to departure from Cairo (8 October), contact your Egyptian bank (CIB, National Bank of Egypt, Banque Misr, QNB, HSBC, ADIB) to set a Travel Notice. Ensure your international spending limits are active to prevent automated fraud security holds.

Terminal Currency Choice (DCC Tip)

When paying by card at Belgian stores, the card terminal may ask: "Charge in EUR or EGP/USD?" Always choose EUR (€)! Choosing EGP triggers high Dynamic Currency Conversion fees (up to 6% markup) by the local terminal merchant.

Cash Strategy (€50 – €100)

Belgium is a 95%+ cashless society. Carrying €50 to €100 in cash is more than sufficient for small historic street snacks, souvenir stalls in Ghent, or discretionary personal tips. ATMs (Bancontact / Euronet) are readily accessible near the hotel.
